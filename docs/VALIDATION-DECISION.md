# OSEILLE: bounded commercial validation, 22 November–6 December 2026

## Current decision

Continue one limited experiment. Commercial viability is unproven. Do not fund another broad build-out, paid acquisition, subscription stack or four parallel product roadmaps without evidence. Keep the existing public free services and monitoring running; pausing development is not deleting or disabling them.

The four-service infrastructure is functional and a sunk cost, not a reason to continue spending time. No enrolled independent consumer, recurring team or verified revenue has been established. Unknown callers remain unknown. Publishing an invitation or an internal demonstration does not validate a market.

Public alternatives checked on 22 November: npm audit already returns dependency vulnerabilities and remediation information (https://docs.npmjs.com/auditing-package-dependencies-for-security-vulnerabilities/). Socket exposes a hosted MCP server and an upgrade planning command (https://docs.socket.dev/docs/guide-to-socket-mcp and https://docs.socket.dev/docs/socket-fix). These establish overlap, not a market-size estimate or user preference. Our prospective differentiation—small exact-version decision reports usable by agents—remains a hypothesis.

## One offer, one buyer hypothesis

Target: a developer or small team using a coding agent to evaluate a real npm/PyPI dependency update.

Question: “For this exact update, which known advisories change, what remains uncertain, and what must I validate before shipping?”

Free experiment: a local report comparing current and candidate pinned versions through two existing Radar calls. No server feature or new infrastructure. Guardian/Repair remain available for related contract failures. Signals serves a different buyer and receives reliability maintenance during this experiment, rather than another acquisition effort.

The experiment does NOT claim changelog analysis, test execution, transitive dependency auditing, automatic patches or security certification. A sample report is an internal publisher demonstration. It cannot establish time savings, adoption or willingness to pay.

## Time and expenditure boundary

Decision date: 6 December 2026, even if no pilot has started. Do not restart the clock after the first signup. Suggested remaining owner-time ceiling: 6 hours across the experiment; record actual time separately. This is an operating limit, not time already worked or automatic budget enforcement. No additional spending is authorized by this plan. Real payment activation remains a separate readiness decision.

Use the existing daily report and outreach journal. No second monitoring stack. No new product features unless the pilot reveals a specific blocker that fits the remaining time budget.

## Evidence to seek

- Invite ten relevant independent teams through permitted channels, with affiliation disclosed; log delivery and replies. This is a target, not ten contacts already made. No spam or invented recipients.
- Three independent teams complete a real dependency decision, with redacted evidence and voluntary attribution review.
- Two teams return on another day for a second actual decision; repeating the example is excluded.
- At least one writes a specific willingness-to-pay use case and acceptable price. A payment widget click or testnet transfer is not willingness to pay.
- For each task record the previous method, what the result changed, reported time spent, missing evidence and whether they'd use their existing tool instead. Do not infer saved time from HTTP response duration.

## Decision rules on 6 December

CONTINUE: three real teams, two actual repeat decisions and one written paid-use case; proceed to focused premium validation with measured costs. These small thresholds justify another test, not a business-success claim.

CHANGE THE OFFER: teams try it, but results do not change a decision or there is no repeat/willingness to pay. Stop adding adjacent features. Test a narrower need grounded in the replies, within a separately bounded experiment.

PAUSE DEVELOPMENT: after at least ten delivered, relevant invitations, no independent team completes a useful task, or teams consistently say existing alternatives solve it sufficiently. Keep the stable public service available as allowed; avoid further discretionary engineering.

DISTRIBUTION NOT TESTED: fewer than ten relevant invitations delivered because channels or sessions remain inaccessible. Report the reach gap plainly. This does not prove no market; it does justify pausing engineering rather than extending the build indefinitely. A single capped distribution effort is a choice for the owner, not an automatic deadline reset.

STOP / ARCHIVE AS A BUSINESS: if the scoped follow-up still has no specific problem, repeated independent use or credible willingness to pay, stop treating OSEILLE as a revenue project. Preserve code and evidence; use it as a portfolio/learning asset. Never delete customer data or disable free services automatically.

## Instrumentation

Use D1 origin classifications and enrolled opaque team IDs only where consent and review exist. Link voluntary reports to actual successful calls without IP inference. Keep tests, discovery/catalogues, unknowns, first business decisions, second days, written paid intent and actual payments separate. Do not count GitHub stars as buying intent or compute a visitor funnel from unrelated anonymous requests.

Payment readiness is distinct: funded Base Sepolia test payer, full settlement/receipt/result/replay proof, recipient control, actual provider costs and invoice handling. A functioning payment rail does not resolve the product-demand question.
